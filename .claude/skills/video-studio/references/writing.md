# Writing: sound human, be funny, hold attention

- Write to be heard. Short sentences. Contractions. Read every line aloud (or imagine the voice): if you would not say it to a friend, rewrite it.
- One idea per line; one line is 1 to 2 sentences, at most about 20 words.
- Start in the middle: the first line is the most surprising true thing, not an introduction or "In this video".
- Specific beats general: "47 minutes", not "a long time"; a name, a place, an object.
- Open loop: raise a question early ("but that wasn't the strangest part"), answer it later than the viewer expects, never forget it.
- Rhythm: vary line length; a three-word line after a long one lands the point. Pauses are written as line breaks.
- Humour: understatement, the rule of three with a twist on the third, a deadpan aside, a precise odd detail. Do not explain a joke, do not laugh at your own joke, no filler jokes, no dated references. If it is not funny aloud, cut it.
- Documentary tone: calm, confident, curious; let facts carry the drama. Avoid superlatives you cannot source ("the most deadly ever").
- Cut: "basically", "actually", "very", "in order to", "it is worth noting", "let's dive in", "so without further ado".
- End on an image or a turn, then one line that makes the next step easy (watch next, follow). No long outro.
- Numbers: write as spoken ("two thousand and four"); avoid reading more than two numbers in a row.
- Captions: 3 to 6 words per screen, highlight one word, check reading speed (about 3 words per second).
- Local languages and names: only after a native speaker checks them.
